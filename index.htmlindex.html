Pricing Signal

Battery-electric average pricing fell through 2024–25 on lower battery costs — in China, roughly two-thirds of EVs sold at or below comparable combustion prices, even before incentives. That convergence is the biggest driver behind emerging-market EV share overtaking several advanced economies in 2025.

The gap worth watching

The ₹17–19 lakh mid-size electric SUV band has gone from Tata-led to the most contested shelf in the market — Curvv EV, BE 6, Creta Electric, and MG ZS EV all start within roughly ₹1.5 lakh of each other. Tata's response is to leapfrog upward with the Sierra.ev into the ₹20–30 lakh premium band rather than fight on price in the middle. Two open questions worth a client conversation: does stepping up in price cede the mid-size battleground to Mahindra and Hyundai, and does the entry-level stronghold (Nexon EV, Punch EV, Tiago EV) hold enough volume to fund the premium push if it takes longer than planned to pay off.

Porter's Five Forces — India EV Passenger Vehicles
Rivalry
High & rising
Mahindra, Hyundai, MG, and Maruti have all launched competing mid-size EV SUVs within months of each other.
New Entrants
Moderate
Capital intensity and charging-ecosystem needs are real barriers, but global entrants keep testing the market.
Buyer Power
Rising
Price transparency and near-identical specs push buyers to shop on ₹/km of range, not brand loyalty.
Supplier Power
Moderate
Battery sourcing remains concentrated; localization is a direct response to this pressure.
Strategic Read
Tata's move upmarket with the Sierra.ev is the right call if the entry segment holds — but "if" is doing real work in that sentence. The more useful number isn't the 40–45% target, it's how much of the EV-share erosion came from the mid-size band specifically versus entry-level. A client in this seat should ask for a cohort breakdown by price band before signing off on the premium bet.
